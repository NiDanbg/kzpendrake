CHAPTER 1: The Chaos Begins

Quibbletown never slept. Sometimes the city took a quick nap, only to be interrupted by the boom of a botched spell or the shouts of street vendors peddling "fresh" magic potions that carried a barely concealed whiff of raw sewage. The alleys wound into a maze, crossing congested squares and surrounding buildings that had sprung up as if in a fit of magical fever. The streetlamps, powered by eternal-glow spells, flickered to the rhythm of their maintainers' moods. Beneath the cobblestones, the waters sang melancholic melodies, and the air was heavy with the scent of burnt bread, static electricity, and that specific energetic blend known as "urban magic."
In the center of this organized chaos stood Wilfred "Willy" Spellburn—a young wizard with ambitions that vastly exceeded his capabilities. Willy was tall but slouched, as if the weight of the world were pressing him into the dirt. His disheveled hair resembled a nest where a pigeon had conducted a failed architectural experiment. His robe, once navy blue, had faded to an undefinable shade of "wizarding gray." His socks, as always, were mismatched—the left one striped, the right embroidered with little dragons making active attempts to escape the fabric.
Today was the big day.
I'll prove I'm not a complete failure, he repeated to himself.
The Wizard's Guild—that arrogant institution dictating the rules in Quibbletown—had given him a chance. The task seemed elementary: a spatial optimization spell for parking.
The city suffered from traffic jams of epic proportions. Carts, flying carpets, and even randomly animated statues fought for every inch of available space. Citizens routinely resorted to bribery, threats, or petty curses just to snag a spot.
Willy took his position in the central square. Curious onlookers, a few haughty Guild representatives, and a suspiciously large number of pigeons who seemed to sense the impending disaster gathered around him. Archmage Velkor outshone the rest—the golden threads on his robes reflected the light so brightly they blinded the birds and painted little suns on the paving stones. Velkor watched the young man with the expression of a judge who had already signed the verdict and was merely waiting for the tedious formality to conclude.
The young man gripped his magic wand—a cheap model from a street stall, advertised as "barely touched." The wood was slightly warped and occasionally threw off sparks that smelled like burnt toast. Willy straightened his back and cleared his throat.
"Ladies and gentlemen," he began, his voice trembling like the string of an untuned harp. "Today, I will show you a spell that will transform parking in Quibbletown forever!"
The crowd murmured in disbelief.
"Hope it's better than that cleaning charm that turned my laundry into frogs!" someone shouted from the back.
Willy ignored the heckler. He raised his wand, closed his eyes, and began to recite the formula. He had rehearsed it all night, though around midnight he had fallen asleep on the pages, leaving a trail of drool across the chapter titled "Spatial Distortions."
"Parcus Maximus… Spatium Reducto…" he muttered as the instrument in his hand began to vibrate.
The crowd held its breath, expecting something impressive, or at least something that wouldn't set someone's hat on fire.
The first parking space, outlined in glowing paint, flickered. Its colors shifted violently—from blue to green, until they finally settled into an alarming shade of purple. Willy cracked one eye open. This was definitely not part of the plan. With a soft pop, reminiscent of an aged bottle of wine being uncorked, the glowing rectangle vanished. The magic had punctured the spatial barrier in exactly the wrong place, and instead of a cart on the square, something else materialized.
A troll stood before them. Massive, with skin like granite and eyes gleaming like polished quartz. In one hand, he held a ledger; in the other, a magical pencil that looked utterly ridiculous in his massive palm. The troll surveyed the attendees, locked eyes with Willy, and rumbled in a voice like a collapsing avalanche:
"You. Illegal parking in The Rocky Void. Fine: three goats or the currency equivalent in magical crystals."
There was a collective gasp. Someone in the back couldn't hold it in and snickered.
Willy blinked. His confidence was evaporating like water on a hot stove.
"But... that was just a pumpkin cart!"
The troll furrowed his stone brows—a sound akin to shifting river gravel.
"Pumpkins? That incurs an additional fee for biological waste."
He scribbled something in his notepad, and the crowd erupted in laughter. One of the Guild wizards—a tall man with a beard that seemed to possess a life of its own—shook his head and muttered something about a "generation of amateurs." Velkor remained silent, but a spark resembling a hidden smile rippled across the golden threads of his robe.
Willy's face burned.
"Wait!" he cried out, waving his wand in a panic. "I'll fix it!"
A fatal mistake. The wand erupted in a shower of sparks that struck the adjacent parking space. It vanished with a sharp crack, and in its place, a fountain sprouted. Not an ordinary fountain, but a contraption spewing thick chocolate syrup and singing a melody that sounded like the anthem of a forgotten dimension.
The crowd was no longer laughing. They were roaring with delight. The pigeons, who up until now had watched in haughty silence, dove for the chocolate. One of them—larger and with an unusually intelligent gaze—landed next to Willy and squawked:
"That bordered on heroism... if by heroism you mean turning the town square into an interdimensional buffet."
Willy stared, dumbfounded.
"You... you can talk?"
"Of course I can talk," the bird puffed up its chest. "I am Archibald, former wizard, victim of an unfortunate transmutation incident. But you, my boy, just eclipsed even my greatest failures. Congratulations."
Before Willy could reply, the troll chimed in again, his irritation mounting:
"New violation. Operating a fountain without the required permit. Fine: five goats or the equivalent in semi-precious stones."
"I don't have any goats!" Willy screamed, feeling the situation spiraling completely out of control.
He waved the wand once more in a desperate bid for a miracle. A third parking space vaporized. In its place appeared a small dragon, looking simultaneously bewildered and slightly offended. The beast exhaled a pillar of fire, setting a senior wizard's hat ablaze, and the crowd cheered as if this was all part of the scheduled entertainment.
"Enough!" A voice sliced through the clamor like a thunderclap.
Everyone froze. Even the dragon stopped breathing fire and tilted its head in curiosity. From the shadows stepped a figure in a black robe that seemed to swallow the ambient light. A pale face, eyes like two spheres of ice, and an aura that forced even the pigeons to back away. It was Inquisitor Grimble, leader of the Guild of Parking Inspectors—an organization that struck more terror into the hearts of Quibbletown's residents than most dark wizards.
"Wilfred Spellburn," Grimble enunciated, his voice sounding like the slamming of a stone sarcophagus. "Your actions constitute a violation of Article 47, Section 3 of the Urban Magic Code: unauthorized generation of interdimensional portals. You have also incited mass chaos in a public square, a clear violation of Article 12. And most importantly—you have trampled upon the sacred law of parking: never occupy a space without paying the toll."
"But I didn't... I didn't mean to..." Willy stammered. Grimble raised a hand, and the words caught in his throat.
"You have three days to clean up this mess," the Inquisitor continued. "Otherwise, the city will impose sanctions. And sanctions, Mr. Spellburn, are exceedingly unpleasant."
His smile was like a fissure in a glacier—cold and menacing. Velkor offered a barely perceptible bow to Grimble—a brief, formal gesture between colleagues, not enemies. Willy missed the exchange entirely.
The square emptied rapidly. The young man was left alone among the troll, the dragon, the singing fountain, and the talking pigeon. The troll was still taking notes in his ledger, the dragon was trying to lick up the chocolate syrup, and Archibald landed on Willy's shoulder.
"Well, boy, that was impressive. What now?"
Willy surveyed the ruins and felt his stomach tie itself into a knot.
"Now," he murmured, his voice trembling, "I think I need a very strong cup of tea. And probably a new identity."
Deep down, he knew he wouldn't run. Quibbletown was his home, and he had just turned it into an interdimensional parking lot. If he couldn't find a way to repair the damage, Grimble would see to it that his life became unbearably complicated.
Meanwhile, the little dragon had discovered the chocolate fountain and was staring at it with the expression of a creature that had just found the meaning of existence.
